# Light, water and terrain

Choose a consistent imagined light relative to the approach. The player's location defines a point source when explicitly requested. Under an overhang retain a connected dark core with a broken, softer penumbra. Vary reach and intensity gradually along the ledge. Look again under actual game light for double-dark recesses. An exposed ridge can take a few warm highlights while a quiet secondary roof needs very few.

Trace water from a source to a receiver. Start window drips immediately under the sill, strongest at the source. Fade into neighboring stone over a length proportional to wall height and vary the tails. Keep dry neighboring bays calm. A maintained facade can have a few stains around drains, while abandoned masonry can support broader damp patches and vegetation. Treat soot at a chimney and splashback at a footing as different processes.

For cliffs, follow fracture direction, darken a connected crevice and let exposed planes register as a larger mass. For trees, check the gaps between branches before painting leaves. Use clusters of cooler interior foliage and selectively warmer crown tips where the sky reaches them. Where terrain, openings or ownership are inferred, survey first and describe uncertainty. The current Studio masonry executor covers registered towers and bridges.
