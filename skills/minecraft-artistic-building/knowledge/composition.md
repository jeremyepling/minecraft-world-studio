# Massing and rhythm

At the approach distance, reduce the building to its skyline, its wall mass and the gaps around them. A narrow tower can read as taller if its crown is clear of the trees. A large gate reads as an entrance when the dark opening is legible from the road. Walk one offset approach to check for tangencies between peaks, trees and the main roofline.

Choose what dominates, what frames it and where the eye can rest. Use repeated bay widths or a continuous eave to unify a village. Break the rhythm once at the entrance or bend in the street. An extra bay on a taller tower should continue the cadence of its windows. A village addition can share roof pitch and stone temperature while changing orientation or the number of bays.

If the site, dimensions or tool cannot support the requested massing, describe a reachable silhouette or present alternatives. Copy a relationship such as a small warm accent against broad cool stone. Give the new build its own shapes.
